Privacy Policy — Digit Burst

Effective date: October 24, 2026 · Last updated: October 24, 2026

This Privacy Policy describes how Digit Burst (数字风暴 / 數字風暴; the “Game”) and its independent developer (“we,” “us,” or “our”) handle information. It covers the iOS Game, including its TestFlight builds, the Android version being prepared for Google Play, and this support website. The Android version currently has no Firebase Analytics, Crashlytics or Google Play Games leaderboards. The WeChat mini game 数字爆连 is outside this policy's scope and requires separate privacy disclosures through WeChat. The Game is designed to be playable without a developer-operated account.

Current iOS version: Digit Burst 1.0 for iOS is free. It shows no ads, offers no in-app purchases and does not ask for permission to track you. The Android version being prepared for release is also free, with no ads or in-app purchases. We will update this policy before introducing advertising, purchases or new data-processing services.

1. Local game data and information you provide

The Game stores settings, language preferences, game progress, local scores, gameplay records and related verification or pending leaderboard-submission records on your device. This information is used to run the Game, remember your preferences and maintain your records. To check results before they count, the Game keeps on your device a replay of recent runs (your inputs and their timing, the run's random seed and result), simple timing statistics such as the typical interval between links, and any pending leaderboard submissions, which include your Game Center player identifier. The Game keeps these replay and verification records on your device. A qualifying score and its context may be sent to Game Center as described in Section 4, and optional gameplay summaries may be sent to Firebase as described in Section 5. Local data is not automatically a developer-operated online account. You can generally remove it by deleting the Game or clearing app data where supported; device or cloud backups may retain copies.

We do not require your name, home address, phone number, contacts or precise device location to play. If you email us for support or privacy requests, we receive your email address and the content you choose to send so that we can respond.

The Game offers an optional feedback link to forms hosted by Tally (tally.so). Opening a form leaves the native gameplay interface and loads a third-party webpage. The Game does not attach a game account ID, device identifier, game seed or gameplay record to that link, and does not send the feedback text to Firebase. The form opened matches the Game's language setting. The forms do not ask for your name or email address. If you submit feedback, we can view the text and any other information you voluntarily include. Tally may also process technical information related to visiting and submitting the form, such as browser and usage information, in accordance with its Privacy Notice. Please do not include sensitive personal information. Contact us if you would like us to locate or delete a submission where possible; please tell us roughly when you submitted it and what it said, since the form is not linked to your game.

Sharing a result: the Game generates a result-poster image on your device. The image contains gameplay results, an illustrative number chain, the Boss and a game-installation QR code; it does not include your Game Center name or player identifier. If you choose to share it, the image is passed to the app or destination you select through the system share sheet. The Game does not upload the poster to a developer-operated server or read your contacts or recipient list. The destination service handles the shared image under its own policies.

2. Advertising

The current iOS and Android 1.0 versions show no ads. The iOS version does not start Google Mobile Ads or its advertising-consent flow and does not request App Tracking Transparency permission. The Android version has no advertising SDK. We do not use these versions for cross-app advertising tracking or targeted advertising.

3. Purchases and installation verification

The current iOS and Android 1.0 versions offer no in-app purchases, subscriptions or paid ad removal. On iOS, the Game uses Apple's verified StoreKit app-transaction information to distinguish App Store and TestFlight installations for service availability, analytics build-channel labels and the installation link on shared posters. This does not send your payment details to the developer.

4. Leaderboards and platform services

The iOS Game uses Apple Game Center for optional live leaderboards. When you are signed in to Game Center and a qualifying result is submitted, Apple may process your platform player identifier, display name, leaderboard identifier, result and related game context. Current leaderboard results include total damage for Score Attack or Weekly Challenge, or a Boss Challenge completion time; the leaderboard context records which Boss was played and the run's longest chain. Only results that pass the Game's on-device result check are submitted, and only for the Game Center account that was signed in when the run started. Public leaderboard entries, rankings and Game Center display names may be visible to other Game Center users under Apple's settings and visibility rules. At launch the Game may show Apple's Game Center sign-in; you can dismiss it and play without signing in. Viewing rankings downloads other players' Game Center display names and scores from Apple.

The current Android version has local records only and does not submit scores to Google Play Games Services. The Game does not operate its own player-account service, chat service or leaderboard backend. Signing out of Game Center or uninstalling the Game does not by itself delete a result already held by Apple; manage Game Center data and deletion requests through Apple's available account and privacy tools.

5. Optional gameplay analytics — Firebase Analytics

Supported iOS distribution builds include Google Firebase Analytics for product improvement. Gameplay analytics is off by default in every region. When the service is available, the Game's Home screen shows a one-time privacy card with two separate switches, “Share gameplay analytics” and “Share crash reports”, both off, and a link to this policy. Gameplay analytics is enabled only when you turn on its switch; custom gameplay events are discarded while it is off. The same switches stay in the Game's Settings, where you can change them at any time. The Game's custom gameplay events are sent only while gameplay analytics is enabled and the service is available. Firebase is initialized in App Store and TestFlight builds after Apple's StoreKit verifies the installation, including when both switches are off. Initialization and local diagnostic recording are separate from gameplay-analytics collection and crash-report uploading. Local crash recording is described in Section 6. Development, simulator and automated-test builds do not start the Firebase service.

Events describe: the screens you open (Home, a run, the result, the Boss Collection, rankings, Settings, How to play, Feedback); tutorial start and completion; choosing a Boss or a difficulty; opening the rankings; run start and end, each with the game mode, difficulty, Boss, rules version, how many runs have been started on the device, and a random code created for that run only (it is not stored and only links the events of one run); at run end also the active play time, whether the run was completed or quit, total damage, longest chain, perfect counters, whether the Golden Core was earned, the outcome, the Boss kill time where applicable, whether the result passed the Game's on-device check, and a summary of the run (number of attacks, total chain length, interrupts and counters, how many Boss skills were interrupted or landed, and the result of the twin marks); how each Boss skill cast ended (interrupted, landed or other); the play-again action; and taps on the share button. Each event also records whether the build came from the App Store or TestFlight. These events are for understanding engagement, retention, difficulty balance and reliability. A share tap is not proof that sharing was completed. The Game does not send your touches or finger paths, game seeds, replays, Game Center identity, name, email or feedback text, and does not attach a developer account ID to these events.

When enabled, Firebase may also process SDK-generated information such as an app-instance or installation identifier, device and operating-system details, app version, sessions, approximate geographic information inferred from network data, event timestamps, and events the Firebase SDK records automatically, such as first open, session start, engagement time and app updates. This information may be pseudonymous rather than completely anonymous. The iOS Firebase configuration disables IDFV collection, analytics-based ad-personalization signals and automatic screen reporting. The Game uses the Firebase Analytics library without advertising-identifier (IDFA) support, sets Google's advertising-storage, ad-user-data and ad-personalization consent signals to denied, and sets no user ID; we do not use Firebase Analytics for cross-app advertising tracking. You can turn off gameplay analytics in Settings. The Game disables further analytics collection and requests a reset of its analytics data on withdrawal; this cannot recall data already sent to Google.

6. Optional crash reporting — Firebase Crashlytics

Supported iOS distribution builds also include Google Firebase Crashlytics to help diagnose crashes. Its “Share crash reports” choice is off by default and independent of gameplay analytics and advertising consent. Automatic crash-report uploading is disabled in the Game. After Firebase is initialized, Crashlytics may record crash diagnostics locally on your device even while this switch is off. With your opt-in, previously unsent reports stored on the device may be sent on a subsequent launch; without opt-in, the Game requests deletion of unsent reports when it makes the upload decision. Switching the option off stops further authorized sending and requests deletion of unsent reports, but cannot retract reports already transmitted or queued for transmission. Turning on “Share crash reports” authorizes sending pending reports at the next launch. Turning it off also requests deletion of pending reports in the current session; it cannot recall reports already queued or sent.

Crash reports may contain stack traces, app and operating-system versions, device model and technical characteristics, timestamps, session information, Crashlytics/Firebase installation identifiers, whether the build came from the App Store or TestFlight and, if gameplay analytics is also on, recent analytics events recorded before the crash. Such data helps diagnose and prioritize faults. We do not intentionally add your name or the contents of optional Tally feedback to crash reports. For Firebase service practices, see Privacy and Security in Firebase.

7. Purposes, service providers and disclosures

We use information to operate gameplay and local records, provide optional Game Center leaderboards, diagnose problems, check results on-device, analyze optional gameplay metrics, and respond to feedback or support requests. Relevant providers are Apple for Game Center and installation verification, Google Firebase for optional iOS analytics and crash reports, Tally for feedback forms, and GitHub for website hosting. Apps you choose for sharing receive the result image you share. These providers handle information under their applicable terms and privacy policies.

Where required by applicable law, processing is based on performance of the service you request, your consent for optional analytics or crash-report sharing, legitimate interests such as security and responding to requests where legally permitted, or legal obligations. You may withdraw consent for consent-based processing without affecting the lawfulness of earlier processing.

We do not sell personal information to data brokers or share it for targeted advertising in the current versions. We may disclose information if lawfully required or necessary to protect the security of the Game and its users.

8. Retention and security

Local records generally remain until you remove app data or uninstall the Game, subject to device backups. You may ask us to review or delete support emails and feedback we directly control. Game Center records are held by Apple under its retention rules and your platform settings.

Google Analytics is configured with a 14-month retention setting for the user-level and event-level data covered by that control. This is not a deadline for deleting all analytics information: aggregated reports are outside that setting, and user-identifier retention may reset with new activity depending on Google's settings. See Google's data-retention explanation. We do not currently export gameplay analytics to our own BigQuery database. Firebase states that Crashlytics keeps crash traces and associated identifiers for 90 days before starting removal from live and backup systems; deletion is not immediate at day 90.

We delete feedback submissions and support emails when they are no longer needed to respond or improve the Game, and in any case within 24 months. Turning off an optional setting does not automatically erase information previously received by a provider. Tally and GitHub maintain their own technical records under their policies. We use standard security measures and encrypted connections where supported, but no internet-connected service can guarantee absolute security.

9. Your privacy choices and rights

You can change the optional gameplay-analytics and crash-report switches on the iOS Home privacy card and in Settings when those functions are available. Gameplay remains available with both switches off. You can decline Game Center sign-in, choose whether to open a feedback form, and choose whether and where to share a result. Android currently has no Firebase analytics or crash-report switches because those services are not integrated.

Subject to applicable law, you may have rights to request access, correction, deletion, restriction, portability or objection to processing of personal information. You may withdraw consent for consent-based processing without affecting earlier lawful processing, and may have the right to complain to your local data-protection authority. For information controlled directly by the developer, email the contact below with enough detail to locate the record; do not send passwords, payment-card details or unnecessary personal information.

Because the Game has no developer-operated player accounts and does not display your Firebase analytics or crash identifier, an email address alone generally does not let us identify those records. Turning off gameplay analytics stops further analytics collection and resets its local analytics data; it does not by itself delete past server-side records. We will review requests and explain the available steps and any limits. Requests about information held by Apple, Google or another provider may also require using that provider's privacy tools. We will respond as applicable law requires.

10. Children and parental choices

The Game's age rating is shown on the App Store for your country or region. That rating describes content suitability; it does not establish a player's age or provide parental consent for data processing. The Game does not ask for a date of birth and does not provide an in-game verifiable parental-consent process. The optional analytics and crash-report switches are not age verification or verifiable parental consent.

Where applicable law requires a parent's or guardian's consent for optional data processing, that consent is required in addition to any in-game choice. A parent or guardian should keep “Share gameplay analytics” and “Share crash reports” off unless any required consent or other lawful basis has been established. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children under 13, or the applicable local age threshold, without the consent or safeguards required by law. If you believe a child's information has been provided to us, please contact us. We will review the request, address information we control as required by law, and explain available steps for information held by platform providers.
